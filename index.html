Credit Stress-Testing Engine

An end-to-end credit stress-testing pipeline on LendingClub data that links loan-level default risk to the macroeconomy: a PD model → macro satellite → scenario engine. It estimates a fixed-horizon probability of default, calibrates it out-of-time to observed portfolio losses, isolates a robust macro-to-loss transmission channel, and transmits historical recession paths into stressed default and expected-loss figures.

The project is model‑risk‑aware: the focus extends beyond predictive modeling and the final loss estimate to include leakage control, out‑of‑time validation, calibration, stability testing, and the diagnostic process that corrected an earlier flawed lifetime‑PD design to arrive at the final fixed‑horizon framework.

Project Highlights

  • Built a leakage-safe 24-month probability-of-default model. Validated the PD model on true out-of-time vintages, with OOT AUC = 0.707 and stable validation/OOT performance.
  • Intercept recalibration reconciled predicted PD to the observed ~13.8% book default rate.
  • Built macro satellite model that estimates an actual-to-expected default multiplier as a function of macro conditions. Isolated a significant, correctly-signed unemployment channel that is robust across five specifications that add credit spreads, GDP growth, and time trend.
  • Result: a +1 percentage point larger rise in unemployment increases the A/E default multiplier by about 22.6%, stable across five specifications.
  • Transmitted calm, adverse, and severely adverse stress paths through the satellite model, using historical unemployment paths from the 2001 recession and the 2008–09 GFC.
  • Result: a GFC-paced unemployment shock raises annual expected loss by about 36%, or roughly +$21.4 million per $1 billion of exposure, through the unemployment channel alone.
  • Model-risk journey: an earlier terminal-status lifetime-PD design generated a spurious late-window loss escalation, which was traced to survivorship/truncation bias and corrected by rebuilding the outcome definition around a fixed 24-month horizon.

Credit Card Fraud Detection

Binary classification for rare-event financial risk detection — modeling fraudulent transactions on a severely imbalanced dataset (0.172% positive class). The same methodological challenges (class imbalance, threshold selection, AUC-based evaluation) underpin credit default and PD modeling.

  • Addressed severe class imbalance via sample weighting and balanced class settings — central to default and fraud risk modeling
  • Stratified train/test split with feature standardization and L1 normalization
  • Compared Decision Tree and SVM classifiers; evaluated with ROC-AUC, the standard metric for imbalanced credit-risk problems
  • Results: Decision Tree AUC 0.966, SVM AUC 0.984, with explicit attention to the cost asymmetry of false negatives

PhD Dissertation

Essays on Wholesale Banking and the Macro-Economy

My doctoral dissertation studies how the financial sector transmits shocks to the real economy. It examines the rise of wholesale (market-based) banking since the 1980s, the role of financial regulation in that growth, and how macroeconomic uncertainty propagates through bank leverage and balance sheets to aggregate investment and output. The work combines structural theory with empirical estimation across two chapters.

Chapter 1: A Structural Model of Banking, Regulation, and Volatility

A general-equilibrium (DSGE) model that distinctly represents two bank types — retail and wholesale — differing in their leverage constraints, calibrated to U.S. Flow of Funds data to explain the post-1980s growth of wholesale banking and its consequences for business-cycle volatility.

  • Modeled financial regulation as a structural parameter — an interbank-market friction on wholesale banks — and ran a deregulation experiment calibrated to real policy change (the Glass-Steagall repeal / Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act of 1999).
  • Result: interbank deregulation alone raises the wholesale sector's asset share from ~25% to ~37%, and full deregulation to ~45% — quantifying how the regulatory environment reshaped the structure of the banking system.
  • Showed that a larger wholesale sector amplifies the link between the financial and real economy, producing a more volatile aggregate economy under risk shocks.

Chapter 2: An Empirical Analysis on Uncertainty, Bank Leverage, and the Real Economy

A structural vector autoregression (VAR) on quarterly U.S. data (1986–2019) identifying the effect of aggregate uncertainty shocks on the financial sector and real activity.

  • Estimated a 10-variable VAR with bank balance-sheet variables for both banking sectors, identifying uncertainty shocks (VXO) via Cholesky decomposition, with confidence bands from Monte Carlo simulation.
  • Result: higher macroeconomic uncertainty significantly tightens bank leverage and shrinks assets — sharply for wholesale banks, negligibly for retail — which then reduces aggregate investment and output.
  • Established the wholesale banking sector as the primary channel through which uncertainty transmits to the real economy, underscoring the value of modeling banking sub-sectors separately.

Relevance to financial-sector risk modeling: This dissertation examines how exogenous shocks are transmitted through bank balance sheets to macroeconomic outcomes, and how leverage and regulation shape that transmission. These questions align closely with the analytical foundations of stress testing, scenario design, and systemic-risk analysis.
